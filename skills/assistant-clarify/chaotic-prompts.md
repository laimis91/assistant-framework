# Clarify Compressed or Mixed Requests

Reflect the likely goal briefly, using the user's vocabulary. Separate mixed outcomes, priorities, and constraints only as much as needed to identify the next action. A provisional brief can help when the user asks for structure; it is not a required response form.

Before asking, inspect the conversation and available source. Ask every unresolved material question that lacks an evidence-backed safe default. Group related decisions and explain what changes and the risk if guessed. Offer recognizable choices or a recommendation instead of “Can you clarify?” Avoid a numeric question quota.

Examples:

- “Should the primary deliverable be the router fix or the research note? The choice changes which files I inspect first. I recommend the fix first given the demo deadline.”
- “Should this be a reusable installed skill, a one-off prompt, or both?”

A recommendation is not consent. For a proposed product choice, wait for an explicit answer before dependent work. Reversibility, convention, and plausibility alone do not establish intent. A technical default is safe only when supported by the user's intent, context, policy, or existing behavior.

After each reply, retain answered decisions and reassess open ones. Do not silently resolve unanswered choices; expose newly material questions. Continue clear independent discovery or authorized work. Once material ambiguity is resolved, proceed without requiring another confirmation or a rigid status/brief envelope.

Keep the exchange concrete and calm. Do not shame the user, repeat their whole prompt, or turn clarification into a lecture. Never request secrets or unapproved external sharing to fill a context gap.
