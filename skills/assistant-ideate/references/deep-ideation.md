# Broad Ideation

Use when broad exploration is requested or necessary to compare a wide option space. Generate varied options before selecting favorites. Useful techniques include inversion, analogy, combining existing capabilities, scale changes, stakeholder lenses, and subtraction. Consider an unconventional approach when it exposes a real alternative; do not add novelty for its own sake.

Keep hard constraints and prior attempts visible throughout. If exploring a relaxed constraint, label that branch and explain what would need to change. Do not re-propose a rejected approach unchanged.

Compare options using criteria relevant to the decision: expected benefit, feasibility, fit with goals, cost, risk, and evidence strength. Explain rankings in plain language. Use scores or weights only when requested or when they make a difficult comparison clearer; justify them and do not imply numerical precision from guesses.

Refine promising candidates with how they work, dependencies, risks, and the smallest validation step. Stop when the comparison answers the request, without filling idea or candidate quotas. Offer selection, combination, or further exploration when useful; do not force a full decision ritual. Capture only the user's actual choice and do not start implementation without authorization.
