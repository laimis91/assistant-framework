# Expert-method design

Use this detail during existing DESIGN work when authoring new or materially
changed capability guidance whose quality depends on expert judgment. Mechanical
and preference-only edits do not trigger it. Reuse adequate existing cases,
methods and verification evidence before collecting more; this adds no phase,
report, schema or expert mode.

## Recover the decision method

Start from existing `success_criteria` and a consequential decision where an
inexperienced approach can produce a plausible wrong outcome. Inspect a
representative expert case or authoritative method. Preserve its source identity
and distinguish observed conduct, the source's recommendation, and your
inference. Role labels, expert vocabulary and a longer procedure are not evidence;
an evidence-free model proposal remains a candidate to check.

Trace the decision from the raw inputs. Identify the cue that matters, the method
it selects or rules out, the action that follows, and the artifact or observation
that method should produce. Retain decision-relevant uncertainty, alternative
explanations and conditions under which the method is inappropriate. Preserve
permission, tool and access limits. Choose sufficient representative cases for
the consequential decisions; impose no case, hypothesis or step quota.

Use accessible evidence already supplied or retained when adequate. Missing
material evidence limits the design and any improvement claim; do not invent
expert facts or require external research for every edit. Ask only for unresolved
consequential source or outcome gaps.

## Check the plausible wrong outcome

Against the existing criteria, choose an independent check capable of rejecting
a plausible incorrect result: expected observations, adverse cases or external
evidence. A check that merely repeats the authored prose is circular. Reuse
canonical verification selection and evidence outputs when those have a consumer;
do not create a separate authoring report. Source inspection and a plausible
method do not establish behavioral gain. Match each claim to the evidence actually
available.

For example, rising request latency alone cannot select database tuning. A
diagnosis method could compare queue or lock wait with query duration, then vary
suspected contributors independently. The expected artifact is a timing
comparison locating the delay; a real query-delay control can reject an incorrect
upstream-wait conclusion. This illustrates a method hypothesis, not a claim that
all latency problems share a cause or that this authoring procedure improves
creator behavior. Use controls appropriate to the actual system and authorization.

## Keep only reusable guidance

Put reusable cue-to-method decisions and their limits in the target instructions;
move supporting detail to references when useful. Preserve the target's metadata,
dependencies, read-only operations, permissions and existing approval gates. Do
not install tools, exaggerate targets or extend persistence to support the method.
More procedure or expert vocabulary alone is not an improvement.

## Source rationale and limits

[Felix Rieseberg's article](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/)
motivates using field methods, raw evidence and external checks.
[FRA cognitive task analysis](https://railroads.dot.gov/human-factors/elearning-attention/cognitive-task-analysis)
describes incident-based elicitation of expert cues and judgments. These sources
motivate an authoring hypothesis; they do not establish best-expert equivalence,
measured framework benefit or causal gains from this creator procedure.
