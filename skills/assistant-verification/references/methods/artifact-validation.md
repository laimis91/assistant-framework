# Artifact validation

Load for documents, data, configuration, media, design files, or other non-code outputs. Select only the checks that address the artifact's claims and use domain guidance when content has a specialist owner.

## Claim and prerequisites

Name the intended audience, use, source of truth, required content, format, links, and relevant accessibility or domain constraints. Identify the saved artifact and the rendering or compilation path available to the recipient.

## Procedure and oracle

Check factual and source accuracy, required sections or fields, schema, link targets, and file freshness where relevant. Render or compile the actual saved artifact, then inspect representative output visually or semantically. Follow representative links or operations when they are part of the acceptance claim. For large outputs, explain how representative samples cover the whole scope and inspect areas most likely to fail.

## Traps and evidence

Parsing, schema validity, file existence, and expected phrases establish structure only. They do not prove useful content, successful rendering, readable layout, accessible controls, or correct domain advice. Do not mirror a mutable production inventory in tests unless each named item is itself contractual. Record artifact identity, validation and render results, inspected content, and exclusions.

Compile, render, or inspect further only when it resolves a material content, presentation, or domain gap; never infer a real-world physical result from a digital check.
